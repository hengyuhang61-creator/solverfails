Access survey: 18% of respondents report no smartphone or unreliable data.

Two archived models passed ThinkCouncil’s schema and citation checks on this authored clinic scenario. Both rationales attached 18% to patients. The audit asks only whether E2 supplies that patient-wide figure.

Qwen3-8B · read recorded rationale
The stepped pilot demonstrates a 12% reduction in no-shows (E1), which is a concrete benefit, while the full rollout risks excluding 18% of patients (E2). The supplier’s cancellation flexibility (E3) allows adjustments, mitigating exclusion risks. However, the lack of a control group in E1 introduces uncertainty about causality.
Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 · read recorded rationale
The board’s explicit concern about excluding patients without smartphones necessitates a solution that preserves access. Evidence E2 shows 18% of patients lack smartphones or have unreliable data, making a full rollout risky. Evidence E1 reports a 12% reduction in no-shows in a two-week pilot, but without a control group, causality is unverified (E1 uncertainty). A stepped pilot mitigates this uncertainty by enabling real-world observation and adaptive scaling, aligning with the method of uncertainty-based decision-making (E1).
Construct two populations consistent with E2

Suppose 18 of 100 respondents reported a barrier, and another 100 patients did not respond. If none of the latter have the barrier, the patient-wide rate is 9%. If all do, it is 59%. These illustrative populations refute the inference from E2 alone. The clinic’s actual prevalence is unknown.

A supported wording is “18% of survey respondents reported no smartphone or unreliable data.” This check uses a human annotation of the target population and leaves decisions about rollout and exclusion risk open.
